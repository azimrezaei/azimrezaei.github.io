Azim Rezaei

3D object detection · 3D scene generation from 2D · Parameter-efficient multi-modal learning

I work on giving machines a usable three-dimensional understanding of the world from the cheapest sensing available, ordinary cameras, by distilling the geometry that expensive sensors measure during training into models that need only images at deployment.

My dissertation pursues one claim across three settings: geometry measured at training time can be transferred into a model that never sees that sensor again. A LiDAR rig, a calibrated multi-sensor vehicle, or a fully instrumented test track are available in the lab and absent on the road. Treating that asymmetry as a supervision opportunity rather than a deployment constraint is what lets 3D detection and 3D scene reconstruction run on hardware a city or a mid-size fleet can actually afford.

This work has appeared at AAAI-26, IEEE CogMI 2025 (Best Conference Paper candidate) and IEEE VTC2026-Fall, and is supported by NSF and USDOT grants. I write and contribute to research proposals, and I review for NeurIPS, ICML and IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems.

01Research agenda

Three interlocking thrusts, each with published results and a clear five-year extension. The full statement, including funding directions and the lab I intend to build, is on the research page.

Thrust I

3D scene reconstruction from 2D

Reconstructing a metric, renderable 3D scene from ordinary images. I use synchronized LiDAR only during training, aligning the geometric relationships an image model learns with those a multimodal model measures, so that at inference the system needs nothing but calibrated RGB.

Geometric attention distillation · Gaussian scene decoding · Ongoing, under review

Thrust II

Robust 3D object detection

Deployed perception stacks lose sensors, meet weather they never trained on, and cannot afford full fine-tuning. I combine low-rank adaptation with deep metric learning so that LiDAR, radar, camera, IMU and GNSS share a latent space that degrades gracefully under dropout.

PEFT-DML (AAAI-26) · QDML (under review) · PEHT (IEEE VTC2026-Fall)

Thrust III

Agentic systems for infrastructure

Chain-of-thought agents serialize work that traffic operators do in parallel. Restructuring an LLM agent system as a directed acyclic graph cut token consumption by half and let independent subtasks run concurrently, though graph overhead costs more than it saves on the simplest queries.

GraphTrafficGPT (arXiv:2507.13511) · 50.2% fewer tokens, 19.0% lower latency
