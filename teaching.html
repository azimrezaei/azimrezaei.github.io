Teaching & service

Teaching

Course design and instruction across two countries, from introductory programming to graduate software engineering.

Philosophy

I designed and taught university courses in Tehran before moving into industry, and I spent six years building machine learning systems in production before returning to a Ph.D. That sequence shapes how I teach: I want students to meet a problem before they meet the technique that solves it.

In practice that means introducing a topic through a case where the obvious approach breaks down, so the algorithm arrives as a resolution rather than as a definition to memorize. A student who has watched a method fail understands its assumptions in a way that a student who only saw it succeed does not.

It also means teaching from real artifacts wherever I can. Production data is messy, labels leak, class distributions are badly skewed, and a model that looks strong on a validation split can fail the moment conditions shift. Students should see that version of the work alongside the cleaned version in the textbook, because it is the version they will be hired to handle.

Three commitments follow. Assessment should reward reasoning, so I give substantial credit for a correct diagnosis of a wrong answer. Students should write, because an engineer who cannot explain a design decision in prose cannot defend it in a design review. And availability outside class matters at least as much as lecturing. Having completed my degrees in three countries, I am aware how much of a program gets navigated outside the syllabus, and how uneven access to that help can be.

Grant writing

Proposal writing has been part of my assistantship from the beginning. I have developed research ideas into fundable problem statements, drafted technical narratives and work plans, and prepared budgets and justifications alongside my advisor for proposals in intelligent transportation and machine learning. My research is supported by the National Science Foundation (CNS-2543209, CNS-2318725) and by the U.S. Department of Transportation through the CYBER-CARE Tier-1 University Transportation Center (69A3552348332). Reviewing for major venues has sharpened this: having evaluated other people's technical arguments under time pressure, I write my own to survive that reading. I intend to be submitting proposals as PI from my first year.
