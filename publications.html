    Robust Multi-Modal 3D Perception under Sensor Dropout via Quantization-Aware Parameter-Efficient Deep Metric Learning (QDML)

    Abdolazim Rezaei, Mehdi Sookhak

    Under review at a major computer vision conference, 2027

    Multi-modal 3D object detection combines cameras, LiDAR and radar, but existing methods generally assume complete sensor availability, rely on full model fine-tuning, and degrade noticeably when one or more sensing modalities become unavailable. QDML is a quantization-aware, parameter-efficient deep metric learning framework for robust 3D detection under arbitrary sensor dropout. It learns a shared embedding space that preserves semantic consistency across sensing modalities, so that available sensors compensate for missing observations during adaptive feature fusion, and it combines parameter-efficient fine-tuning with geometry-preserving quantization-aware training to cut adaptation cost while maintaining the discriminative structure of the representation after INT8 compression. Experiments on nuScenes and KITTI show consistent robustness gains under sensor dropout with substantially fewer trainable parameters.

    Adaptive Prompt Learning for Privacy-Preserving via Vision-Language Models

    Abdolazim Rezaei, Mahboobeh Haghparast, Ahmad Patooghy, Mehdi Sookhak

    IEEE Transactions on Big Data (under revision)

    Roadside cameras in intelligent transportation systems capture scenes containing personally identifiable information, and common mitigations such as blurring and masking remain vulnerable to reconstruction attacks while degrading semantic utility. Existing image-to-text frameworks address this trade-off only partially, since they depend on fixed expert modules and static, manually curated prompt sets that do not generalize across real-world traffic scenes. This paper proposes Adaptive Prompt Learning (APL), which generates textual descriptions of traffic scenes through three complementary feature branches: global-invariant features, instance-dependent features, and fine-grained evidence features for spatially precise evidentiary extraction. Descriptions are iteratively refined against a DMV-aligned scenario bank through a rule-based engine, enabling interpretable and legally transparent violation detection without exposing raw visual data. Code, scenario definitions and experimental configurations are publicly released.

    PEFT-DML: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Deep Metric Learning for Robust Multi-Modal 3D Object Detection in Autonomous Driving

    Abdolazim Rezaei, Mehdi Sookhak

    The 40th Annual AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 2026

    PEFT-DML is a parameter-efficient deep metric learning framework for robust multi-modal 3D object detection. Unlike conventional models that assume fixed sensor availability, it maps diverse modalities — LiDAR, radar, camera, IMU and GNSS — into a shared latent space, enabling reliable detection under sensor dropout and for unseen modality–class combinations. Integrating Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and adapter layers delivers substantial training efficiency while improving robustness to fast motion, weather variability and domain shift. Experiments on nuScenes demonstrate superior accuracy.

  • VTC2026

    Parameter-Efficient Hybrid Transformer (PEHT) for Network Traffic Prediction via Dynamic Urban Congestion Integration

    Abdolazim Rezaei, Mehdi Sookhak, Mahboobeh Haghparast

    IEEE 104th Vehicular Technology Conference (VTC2026-Fall), 2026

    Accurate network traffic prediction underpins resource allocation in dense urban cellular environments, but city dynamics make it hard. PEHT separates primary network communication features from secondary city-traffic features and applies Low-Rank Adaptation within the Transformer encoder, sharply reducing trainable parameters while maintaining predictive accuracy. A multi-modal fusion strategy injects external traffic features at the decoder. Experiments on the Telecom Italia Milan dataset and multiple synthetic congestion scenarios show PEHT outperforming state-of-the-art models on RMSE, MAE and R².

  • VTC2026

    MINT-V2X: A Mobility-Integrated Network Trajectory Dataset for Predictive Resource Management

    Abdullah Anjum, Abdolazim Rezaei, Mehdi Sookhak

    IEEE 104th Vehicular Technology Conference (VTC2026-Fall), 2026

    V2X research needs datasets that pair vehicle trajectories with realistic wireless network parameters, but public datasets almost always provide one or the other rather than an integrated view. MINT-V2X closes that gap by coupling SUMO traffic dynamics with OMNeT++/Simu5G network simulation, validated through 14 standardized tests drawn from 3GPP Release 14 (C-V2X), ETSI standards and Shannon capacity theory. The release contains 9.87 million synchronized data points from 1,386 vehicles and 15 roadside units across three hours of urban traffic simulation, with strong network-metric correlations (CQI–SINR 0.993, SINR–PDR 0.946). An RSU load-prediction case study shows that trajectory data yields better predictive performance than network-history-only baselines. The dataset, experiments and full SUMO configurations are released publicly.

    RL-MoE: An Image-Based Privacy Preserving Approach In Intelligent Transportation System

    Abdolazim Rezaei, Mehdi Sookhak, Mahboobeh Haghparast

    2025 IEEE 7th International Conference on Cognitive Machine Intelligence (CogMI), pp. 93–102

    Vehicular computing enables real-time data collection for traffic flow, mobility and safety, but the proliferation of roadside cameras and IoT devices raises serious privacy concerns. RL-MoE combines Reinforcement Learning with a Mixture of Experts model: instead of transmitting raw images, it extracts textual descriptions from camera feeds. The MoE component uses specialized experts to generate summaries from structured prompts while the RL module optimizes those descriptions for accuracy and relevance, with explainability methods included for transparency. Results show effective privacy protection, improved transmission efficiency and support for compliance with privacy standards.

    GraphTrafficGPT: Enhancing Traffic Management through Graph-Based AI Agent Coordination

    Nabil Abdelaziz Ferhat Taleb, Abdolazim Rezaei, Raj Atulkumar Patel, Mehdi Sookhak

    arXiv preprint arXiv:2507.13511, 2025

    LLM-based traffic management systems such as TrafficGPT rely on chain-based architectures that process tasks sequentially, reload context at each step and handle one query at a time. GraphTrafficGPT restructures this around a directed acyclic graph: tasks become nodes, dependencies become edges, and independent tasks execute in parallel. A centralized Brain Agent handles query decomposition, dependency analysis and agent coordination, dispatching to six specialized agents covering data retrieval, traffic analysis, visualization, SUMO-based simulation, workflow optimization and general queries. Against the original TrafficGPT, the architecture reduced token consumption by 50.2%, improved average response latency by 19.0%, and supported simultaneous multi-query execution with efficiency gains up to 23.0%, though simple visualization tasks, where graph-construction overhead exceeds the parallelization benefit, saw latency increases of 36–40%.

    Privacy-Preserving in Connected and Autonomous Vehicles Through Vision-to-Text Transformation

    Abdolazim Rezaei, Mehdi Sookhak, Ahmad Patooghy, Shahab S. Band, Amir Mosavi

    arXiv preprint arXiv:2506.15854, 2025

    Connected and autonomous vehicles depend on devices that process privacy-sensitive data, particularly AI-equipped roadside cameras used for applications such as violation detection. Face blurring and obfuscation leave individuals trackable through other features such as clothing. This paper introduces a privacy-preserving framework using feedback-based reinforcement learning and vision-language models to convert images into semantically equivalent textual descriptions, retaining scene-relevant information while preserving visual privacy. A hierarchical RL strategy iteratively refines the generated text for semantic accuracy and privacy. Evaluation shows Unique Word Count increasing by approximately 77% and Detail Density by around 50% relative to existing approaches.

    Credit Card Fraud Detection Using Tree-Based Algorithms for Highly Imbalanced Data

    Abdolazim Rezaei, Mohsen Yazdinejad, Mehdi Sookhak

    2024 IEEE 3rd International Conference on Computing and Machine Intelligence (ICMI), pp. 1–6

    Cybercriminals exploit phishing information and manipulate cardholder account balances to appropriate funds in ways that resist tracing. This study addresses detection of fraudulent banking transactions in account data characterized by highly imbalanced fraud records. Across a range of machine learning algorithms with a focus on tree-based approaches, LightGBM and XGBoost show superior performance, with LightGBM notably dominant, reaching an AUC of 0.94649.
